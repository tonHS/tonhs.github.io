Retail Loyalty App  ·  PIPEDA · CASL · Quebec Law 25

"The App Can Launch — But Not Like This"

Five issues required fixing before build — including an illegal CASL consent flow and missing vendor data protection terms that needed action before contracts closed.

Assessment of a proposed retail loyalty app collecting location, payment history, and behavioural data from hundreds of thousands of daily customers. Identified 14 compliance gaps across federal and Quebec law, prioritized by urgency, with cross-border transfer requirements flagged for the legal team before vendor contracts signed.

Connected Vehicle Dealership  ·  PIPEDA · Law 25 · BC/AB PIPA

"Foreign Data Flows the Board Didn't Know About"

Risk score 9/9: connected vehicles collecting biometric and location data without customer notice, consent, or vendor contracts — across five provinces and two foreign manufacturers.

Board-commissioned PIA covering connected vehicle data flows, biometric locks, used vehicle data practices, and multi-province compliance gaps across ~50,000 annual customers. Recommended a 90-day privacy program rebuild, a technical audit of both vehicle lines, and immediate suspension of Quebec biometric enrolments pending a mandatory CAI analysis.

Covert Biometric Surveillance  ·  PIPEDA · Criminal Code · Texas CUBI

"Do Not Proceed"

Criminal Code s. 21 exposure (aiding unlawful confinement) cannot be mitigated by any privacy governance control. This finding stopped the operation.

Scope assessment of a proposed covert facial recognition operation in mall corridors spanning Toronto, Ontario and Dallas, Texas. All eight high-risk PIA flags triggered. Identified criminal liability in Canada and statutory violations under Texas CUBI ($25,000 per biometric capture). Operation recommended not to proceed in either jurisdiction in its proposed form.

Canadian Privacy & AI Regulatory Scan

Last updated: September 2, 2026
Federal legislation — no bill tabled: Bill C-27 (CPPA + AIDA) died on the order paper when Parliament prorogued January 6, 2025. As of August 2026, no replacement federal AI or privacy bill has been tabled.
Split approach confirmed: Government has signalled AI regulation will be separated from privacy reform. AIDA is unlikely to return in its previous form. AI is currently governed by existing laws of general application and a voluntary code of conduct for advanced generative AI systems.
Privacy statute signalled but pending: A new privacy statute and companion tribunal bill were signalled in the 2025 federal budget, but neither had been introduced under a confirmed bill number as of May 2026.
Quebec Law 25 in force: Canada's strictest private-sector privacy law requires mandatory PIAs, cross-border transfer assessments, and carries administrative penalties up to $10M or 2% of worldwide turnover.
